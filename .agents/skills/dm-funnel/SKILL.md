# DM Funnel

## When to Use

Pull this when scripting DM outreach or inbound DM handling, diagnosing 47-message conversations that go nowhere, or wiring a comment-to-DM automation funnel. The DM is where the transaction happens — but only after content did the 85% (see `content-to-client`) and identity is regulated (see `sales-identity`).

**Core thesis: DMs are the back room.** The feed and stories are the stage — no transactions there. The DM is the back room where cards get exchanged. **Your DMs are just a door.** A wealthy buyer doesn't need convincing in the DM; they need a link. Performing for the wrong audience produces long DMs; silence is strategy.

## Core DNA (the operators behind this)

- **Sheila Bella** (*The Respect Method*) — "DMs are the back room," the wealthy 3-message DM, "your DMs are just a door / silence is strategy / desperation is loud," romanticize-outreach, and the keyword-comment funnel (comment RESPECT / RX → automation).
- **AI Sales Team repo** (lifted) — cold-DM / problem-first outreach: lead with a prospect-specific observation tied to a problem, low-commitment ask, stop the sequence on any reply.

## Scripts & Patterns

### The wealthy 3-message DM vs the 47-message DM (verbatim, reel DZEiLiksZPR)

| Selling to the poor (47 messages) | Selling to the wealthy (3 messages, done) |
|---|---|
| Answer questions → follow-up questions → voice notes → whole portfolio → price breakdown → payment plan → motivational speech → break-up advice | "**Hey, I'd love to book.**" → "**Here's my link.**" → done |
| You send a paragraph, they send a paragraph — "you're not closing, you're corresponding" | No essays after high school, not at 10pm "like you're applying for a job you already have" |
| "What's included?" → you list every ingredient like a cereal box | "What's included?" → "It's on your website" (the website already did the selling) |
| They leave you unread → you follow up 4 times ("just checking in") | They leave you unread → you do nothing; "desperation is loud, silence is strategy" |
| Close with "let me know!" | Close with a **deadline** — "guess which one gets the booking" |

> "Your DMs are just a door. That's it." · "A wealthy buyer doesn't need convincing. They need a link, and that reason to trust you is positioning and confidence."

### "Your DMs are just a door / silence is strategy"

The DM is not where you build belief — that's content's job (the 85%). The DM converts a pre-sold buyer with a link and a deadline. When they leave you on read: do nothing. "Desperation is loud, silence is strategy." Don't chase with four "just checking in" follow-ups — that reads as anxiety and trains them you have no other clients.

### Romanticize outreach

Treat reaching out as the craft, not the chore — show up to the DM as the leader who made room, not the applicant begging for the job. Closing to the wealthy "feels like you did them a favor, because they know your schedule is full and you made room for them." Bring that energy to every outbound DM.

### Keyword-comment DM funnel (comment RESPECT / RX → automation)

Every reel ends with a keyword CTA: "**comment the word RESPECT**" (or RX). The mechanics:

1. Content carries the 85% and ends with the keyword CTA.
2. Viewer comments the keyword (public comment = social proof + algorithm signal).
3. DM automation (e.g. ManyChat or any comment-to-DM tool) fires the lead magnet or masterclass link into their DMs.
4. The DM is the back room — link first; conversation only if they're a pre-sold buyer who needs the door opened.

This captures the undecided into a list (the lead-magnet-over-discount-popup move) and routes a warm, self-selected lead straight into the back room.

### Cold-DM / problem-first patterns (lifted from the repo)

When the DM is genuinely cold (no prior content relationship), lead with the problem, not the pitch:

- **Personalized observation** tied to a specific, prospect-relevant problem — at least two prospect-specific details. ("I came across [X] while looking at [topic]…")
- **Connect the observation to value** in one sentence — why it matters to what you do.
- **One low-commitment ask** — a 15-minute conversation, or the link. Never a wall of text.
- **Stop the sequence on any reply** (positive or negative). Don't keep firing scheduled touches at someone already talking to you.
- Problem-first beats feature-first: name the pain they feel, not the thing you sell.

## Anti-Patterns (what to refuse)

- Running a 47-message DM — answering follow-ups to follow-ups, voice notes, portfolios, payment plans, motivational speeches. That's corresponding, not closing.
- Listing every "ingredient" in the DM when the website should already have done the selling.
- Chasing an unread DM with four "just checking in" follow-ups. Silence is strategy.
- Closing with "let me know" instead of a link + a deadline.
- Trying to build belief inside the DM. Belief is content's job (the 85%); the DM is just a door.
- Cold DMs that pitch features instead of naming a specific, prospect-relevant problem.
- Continuing an automated/scheduled DM sequence after the prospect has replied.

## Quality Gates

- [ ] DM treated as the back room — link-and-deadline close, not a belief-building marathon.
- [ ] Wealthy 3-message structure used where the buyer is pre-sold ("I'd love to book → here's my link → done").
- [ ] No chasing on read — silence held instead of four "checking in" follow-ups.
- [ ] Close carries a deadline, not "let me know."
- [ ] Keyword-comment funnel wired (CTA → public comment → DM automation → link/lead magnet).
- [ ] Cold DMs are problem-first with ≥2 prospect-specific details and one low-commitment ask.
- [ ] Automated sequence stops the instant the prospect replies.
- [ ] Outreach energy is leader-made-room, not applicant-begging (romanticize outreach).


## Transcript-grounded upgrade
**The DM flow (in order — never skip to the pitch):**
1. **Content proof** — they engage because the content already showed competence (src: ZVrMFJHnm90).
2. **Low-pressure opener** — react to their context, not a template blast.
3. **Pain question** — surface the real problem in their words.
4. **Cost question** — what is that problem costing them (time/money/status).
5. **Fit question** — are they the kind of person this is built for.
6. **Paid-intent clarity** — confirm they're looking to invest, not browse, *before* a call (src: SJTxSML3ZbM).
7. **Booking** — specific slot + instant invite.
8. **Postbooking warm-up** — hand off to `pre-call-show-rate-system` so the call actually shows.

**Hard rules:**
- **Do not pitch in message one.** Message one earns the reply; the offer comes after pain + cost + fit (src: U2w7SQ7NEUQ).
- **Do not book free-help calls.** Qualify paid-intent first; "book only if you're serious" protects the calendar
  and the posture (the agent-run brand only books qualified intent) (src: gHeOiQ1_V5o).
