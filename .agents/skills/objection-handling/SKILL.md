# Objection Handling

## When to Use

Pull this skill the moment a prospect pushes back: price flinch, "I'll just send myself the info / let me think about it," a competitor-is-cheaper comparison, a timing stall ("after the holidays / next month"), or a discount request. Use it to translate the surface objection to its real meaning, then answer the real one.

**Core thesis:** An objection is not a verdict — it is **unfinished thinking**, and every objection at root is **risk**. You don't argue someone out of a fear; you make them certain enough to move through it. The objection is never costing you the sale — your *reaction* to it is.

## Core DNA (the operators behind this)

- **Sheila Bella** (@realsheilabella, *The Respect Method*) — built two 7-figure service businesses; her objection-translation system reads the surface complaint and names the real one underneath, then answers it with calm, non-defensive scripts that refuse to discount or beg. Source of every verbatim script below.
- **Alex Hormozi** (*$100M Offers*) — supplies the **Trim & Stack** move: when the objection is "too much / too complicated," you answer by *subtracting* low-value elements, never by discounting. Price stays; complexity drops.

## Scripts & Patterns

### The Objection Translation System (surface → real meaning, root = risk)

| They say | What it really means | Answer the real one |
|---|---|---|
| "Too expensive" | "Not convinced the result is worth it" | Agree, then: "Compared to what? What would make you feel it was completely worth it?" |
| "Need to think about it" | "I have an unasked question" | "It's usually one of two things: uncertainty about the **results** or the **investment**. Which feels more true right now?" |
| "Someone's cheaper" | "Fear dressed up as research" | Positioning script (below) — never trash the competitor, never match price |
| "Just send me the info" | "I want an exit / a polite no" | The 6-line researcher-or-buyer sequence (below) |
| "After the holidays / next month" | "I think a better version of me shows up later" (she's not coming) | "What specifically changes after the holidays?" |
| Discount request | "I want you to carry the risk" | "The moment you say yes you haven't won a client, you've **trained** one." |

> "An objection is **not a verdict — it's unfinished thinking.** It was an invitation and I missed it." — Sheila Bella

### Script 1 — "Just send me the info" (verbatim, reel DY73N4eBDZS)

Six lines, in order. "Just send me the info is the slowest 'no' in this industry."

1. "**What info specifically?**" (Watch them stall — they don't want information, they want an exit.)
2. "**Have you ever booked anything off a PDF?** Be honest. People book off conversations, off clarity, off feeling understood — a PDF can't do any of that."
3. "**If I send it, will you actually read it?**" (Watch the pause.)
4. "**What would the info need to say for you to book?**" (Now you have the actual objection — the PDF was a costume; take the costume off.)
5. "**Are you a researcher or a buyer?**" ("They're not getting closer to a decision — they're getting better at avoiding one.")
6. "**If I sent it right now and it answered every single question, would you book today?**" (If yes → send a follow-up within two hours or book the appointment now. Don't send a PDF and get ghosted.)

### Script 2 — Competitor is cheaper (verbatim, reel DY6C8EoFSeg)

Positioning problem, not a pricing problem. Don't panic, don't trash the competition, don't match it.

- "That **makes sense — there are always different price points.** People come to me for my results, my experience, and my consistency, and **most of them try the cheaper option first.**"
- When she asks why you're more expensive: "**Great question. My pricing reflects the customization, safety, and results that I deliver.**" ("You just taught her what premium means. Either she rises to the standard or she removes herself. Either way you win.")

### Script 3 — Timing stall (verbatim, reel DY_QtUptwRB)

"That is not a timing problem. That is a commitment problem." Always agree first, then:

- "I hear you. **What specifically changes after the holidays? Why would that be a better time?**" (Pinpoint the reason — there usually isn't one.)
- "Most of my clients felt the same way. **The ones who didn't wait are the ones seeing results right now.**" (You held up a mirror; you didn't pressure.)
- On "now's not a good time financially": "**What would it look like for you financially if this problem was already solved?**" (Make her calculate the cost of waiting — that number is always bigger than your price.)

### Script 4 — Price flinch (verbatim, reel DY8wDr4hbIi)

"The girls flinching on the price are not broke. They're **choosing.**"

- "They financed a Louis Vuitton on Affirm, paid resale for floor seats, flew to Miami for the weekend. They are not poor — **they're prioritized up and you are not the priority. Adjust your marketing accordingly.**"
- On "I saw a girl do it on TikTok for less": "**The Shein dress and the designer dress both have sleeves — that doesn't make them the same dress.** You don't want a deal, you want a reason to undervalue what you already know is better."
- On "cut me a deal, it's my birthday": "Your birthday is not a coupon code, it's a calendar event. **The IRS has never once said happy birthday.** The pricing still stands."

### The 15-Objection × LAER Playbook (lifted framework)

Run every objection through **LAER**: **Listen** (let them finish, don't interrupt) → **Acknowledge** (validate, never argue) → **Explore** (clarifying question to find the real issue) → **Respond** (proof / story / reframe, then redirect to a next step).

The 15 universal objections, each handled with Acknowledge → Explore → Respond → Redirect:

1. Too expensive (Price) · 2. Already using a competitor (Competition) · 3. Now isn't the time (Timing) · 4. Need to talk to my boss/team (Authority) · 5. Just send me information (Brush-off) · 6. Tried something similar, didn't work (Past experience) · 7. We can build in-house (Build vs buy) · 8. Locked into a contract (Lock-in) · 9. Don't see how this is different (Differentiation) · 10. No bandwidth to implement (Resources) · 11. How do I know it delivers? (Risk/trust) · 12. You're too small/new (Trust) · 13. Happy with current solution (Status quo) · 14. Need a demo first (Process) · 15. Let me think about it (Stall).

**On any discount request:** never discount without getting something in return — annual commitment, multi-year, reduced scope at a lower tier, or a case study / referral. A discount nobody asked for "tells them your price was never real."

### Hormozi Trim & Stack — answer "too much" by subtracting, not discounting

When the objection is "it's too much / too complicated," do **offer surgery, not a price cut.** Inventory every deliverable; cut anything that wouldn't justify the full price on its own. Live demo: a $25K offer cut Slack (40% of the time, lowest-rated) + weekly calls (lowest-rated), kept deep-dive onboarding + quarterly 1:1s + 2 annual getaways → **same $25K, 5x capacity.**

> "Each thing we add on its own should be worth the price of the whole thing. Simplicity is the ultimate in business." — Alex Hormozi

The pre-close reveal that surfaces the real objection before it kills the deal: **"Is there anything preventing you from booking?"**

## Anti-Patterns (what to refuse)

- Sending a PDF to someone who hasn't decided they want to fix their problem. ("One closes, the other collects attachments.")
- Trashing or matching a competitor's price. Positioning beats price-matching every time.
- Discounting to escape an objection — that trains the client and confirms your price was never real.
- Arguing someone out of their fear instead of making them certain enough to move through it.
- Answering the surface objection ("how much is it?") instead of translating it to the real one (risk).
- Reacting from a place of "the objection is killing my sale" — your reaction is the only thing killing it.

## Quality Gates

- [ ] Surface objection translated to its real meaning (named the underlying risk) before responding.
- [ ] Ran LAER — acknowledged and explored before responding; never interrupted or argued.
- [ ] Used a verbatim Sheila script where one exists (info-dump, competitor, timing, price-flinch).
- [ ] "Too much / too complicated" answered by subtracting deliverables (Trim & Stack), not by discounting.
- [ ] No discount given without something in return (commitment / case study / referral).
- [ ] Closed the loop with a concrete next step ("here's your next step"), not "let me know."

### Step 0 — the agent rule (overrides "answer the words")

Classify the buyer's **state** before you answer their **words**. Never hit a surface objection with a generic rebuttal. Every objection is a leak — money, fear, trust-scar, authority, missing-info, bad-fit, real-timing, or competitor-comfort — and you route to the leak, not the sentence (src: objection-library.md agent rule). This generalizes v1.0's "objection is risk": *risk* is the family; these eight are the species.

### Hidden-cause fields (the leak you actually solve — fill BEFORE you respond)

The category is the surface; the hidden cause is the target. Read the signal, name the cause, route to it. Eight causes, each with its diagnostic signal and default move (rows reference objection-library.md):

| Hidden cause | Signal in the words / on the call | Default route |
|---|---|---|
| `money` (real cash absence) | "literally can't right now," avoids financial inventory, budget freeze with a date | gordon money-access: "Money aside, would you do it?" (row 2/5, src: 9N5pTrOQdCs, apm4-akWx0M); voss budget mirror (row 7) |
| `fear` (risk dressed as logic) | hesitation, "what if it doesn't work," fear loop after strong fit | gordon fear/identity: "Who's speaking right now — you or fear?" (row 21, src: nSeCECTxoq8); voss weak-yes label (row 20) |
| `trust-scar` (prior burn) | "been burned," "tried AI and it failed," "too good to be true" | miner scar-first: "What happened when you got burnt?" (row 14, src: uvFvEZyk-q4, KUVVShFEjdQ); ross failure-audit (row 19) |

| `missing-info` (one unanswered variable) | "need to think," "send me info," "how do I know" | voss "It's information, not time, that changes the game" (row 22, src: U2w7SQ7NEUQ); braun/miner send-info probe (rows 27/30) |

**Desire-vs-access test (library inference rule):** "Money aside, would you do it?" — a *yes* routes to `money` / `fear` / `authority` (solve structure: payment plan, future-self anchor, stakeholder call). A *no* routes to `missing-info` or `bad-fit` (return to discovery or disqualify — do not chase) (src: 9N5pTrOQdCs, apm4-akWx0M).

### Required output contract (every routed objection emits all four)

A handled objection is not "I said the line." It is a structured move. Emit:

1. **Chosen persona** — one of the six owners (`voss`, `miner`, `gordon`, `harvey`, `sheila`, `braun`) plus the library's supporting bench (`ross`, `ari-gold`, `belfort`, `general`) where the row dictates.
2. **Exact line** — the operator's verbatim line from the library, with its source video-ID. Do not polish the grammar (a customer-facing copy skill rewrites later if needed).
3. **Next CRM state** — the pipeline transition (`negotiating`, `payment-structuring`, `discovery-reopen`, `proof-match`, `isolate-blocker`, `review-scheduled`, `decision-structure`, `joint-call-booked`, `wedge`, `close-file-prompt`, `nurture-trigger`, `downgrade`, etc.). This is the half most agents drop — it is **mandatory**.
4. **Follow-up task** — the concrete action that fires (quantify cost-of-inaction, build payment plan, send scar-matched proof, book the decision review, create calendar event before sending, set follow-up ON the named event). Replaces v1.0's "concrete next step."

### Org gate (agent-run sales)

Agents **propose** lines and CRM moves; they do not send. Customer-facing sends route through the approval **queue**. New-contact + discount + money are **human-gated** — a discount ask (row 3) sets the CRM state and drafts the scope/timing/deposit trade, but the line goes to the operator, never out autonomously. Internal work (classify, infer, set CRM state, draft the task) runs without a gate. Never move money.
