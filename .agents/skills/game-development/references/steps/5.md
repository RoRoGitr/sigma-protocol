# Game adaptation — Step 5: Wireframe Prototypes

Generated from catalog 1.0.0. Canonical source: `steps/step-5-wireframe-prototypes`.

Treatment: **adapt** — Per-flow prototype PRDs stay (Track A or B). For a game the first prototypes are the cheapest medium that can fail the experience goals: paper or cards for decisions, a greybox with real input latency for feel, beat boards for story, a chat room or tabletop for social play, a blockout for space. Block meshes, greybox levels, a dummy mannequin and temporary audio events are legitimate prototype artifacts. A vertical slice is a later prototype, not the first.

## Creator questions

- **prototype-medium**: Which medium proves the primary uncertainty most cheaply, and why is anything more expensive unnecessary right now?
- **success-signal**: What observable behaviour in a playtest means the prototype passed (players hesitate productively, testers control the object untutored, a stranger finds the exit)?
- **prototype-scope**: What is deliberately left out of this prototype, and which later document owns it?
- **outsider-test**: Who outside the team will play it, and how will the session be recorded?

## inputs

- Flow tree inventories
- Primary proof from ideation
- Prototype media from Step 0

## activities

- Write one prototype PRD per proof (decision, feel, story, social, space) rather than per screen when that is the truer unit
- Specify greybox, block mesh, dummy body, placeholder audio and temp UI as acceptable fidelity
- Plan the playtest protocol for each prototype and its evidence grade
- Run /prd-json --mode prototype afterwards as usual

## reviewCriteria

- Each prototype names the uncertainty it kills and the cheapest medium that can kill it
- Success is an observable behaviour, not a feeling reported by the designer
- No hero art, final score or content volume is required to run the prototype

## revisionRules

- A prototype that fails changes the prototype or the mechanic; changing a goal instead is a recorded creator decision
- A new primary proof adds a prototype PRD rather than expanding an existing one

## Sources and limits

- [Game Design Workshop, 5th Edition - playcentric excerpt](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/book-excerpt-game-design-workshop) — verified-source-text. Author-posted excerpt (2024-04-18) read; the full 5th edition was not read cover to cover. Physical prototypes under-serve real-time feel (see S06). Friend playtests bias results (Fulton, in S08).
- [Game Feel: A Game Designer's Guide to Virtual Sensation](https://www.taylorfrancis.com/books/mono/10.1201/9781482267334/game-feel-steve-swink) — source-claim. Publisher pages and Library of Congress table of contents only; book body not read. Does not apply cleanly to turn-based, menu-driven or non-spatial games. 2008 input-hardware examples.
- [GDC 2012: Sid Meier on interesting decisions](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/gdc-2012-sid-meier-on-how-to-see-games-as-sets-of-interesting-decisions) — verified-source-text. Contemporary write-up read; the GDC video was not frame-reviewed. Meier states rhythm and some puzzle games are a weaker fit. Civilization-heavy examples.
- [The Level Design Book - How to make a level](https://book.leveldesignbook.com/process/overview) — verified-source-text. Process page read. CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 living document, self-labelled under construction and shooter-weighted. Not authored by Steve Lee. Structure may change.
- [Sigma Protocol game-development adaptation (architecture decision and integration contract)](../SIGMA-GAME-ADAPTATION.md) — proposed-adaptation. Our proposal, not an external practitioner method. Nothing here proves a game is good. Every phase's evidence must come from an authorized external experiment and a human review, never from the existence of a document.
