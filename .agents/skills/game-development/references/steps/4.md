# Game adaptation — Step 4: Flow Tree

Generated from catalog 1.0.0. Canonical source: `steps/step-4-flow-tree`.

Treatment: **adapt** — The screen inventory, transitions and zero-omission proof stay and are necessary but insufficient. Games add a space inventory (rooms, routes, fail-forward paths), a verb inventory (what the player can do where), a door or interactable inventory, and a contact-state inventory for controlled bodies (idle, start, stop, turn, jump, land, interact, hit, fail). Puzzle games may be nearly all verbs and no space; open worlds invert that.

## Creator questions

- **space-inventory**: List every space or board the player occupies, its entrances, exits and fail-forward routes. Write 'no spaces' for menu, text or board games and describe scenes instead.
- **verb-inventory**: List every player verb and the first place it is taught.
- **interactable-inventory**: List every interactable class (doors, switches, pickups, NPC talk) that needs a door-problem sheet.
- **contact-states**: If the player controls a body or object in real time, list its locomotion and interaction states. Otherwise write 'no controlled body' and why.
- **recovery-paths**: For every fail state, which screen or space does the player land in, and how do they get back?

## inputs

- UX journeys
- Interaction model
- Verb list from ideation

## activities

- Build the standard screen inventory and transitions
- Add the space, verb, interactable and contact-state inventories with traceability to experience goals
- Prove every verb has a first-teach location and every fail state has a recovery path
- Emit the machine-readable flow tree with the game inventories

## reviewCriteria

- Every verb has a first-teach location
- Every fail state has a recovery
- Every interactable class is listed for a door-problem sheet
- Contact states exist only when a controlled body exists

## revisionRules

- Adding a verb adds a first-teach location and re-opens levels.teaching-fairness
- Removing a space re-opens every level or environment document that referenced it

## Sources and limits

- [The Door Problem](https://web.archive.org/web/20231023231458/https://lizengland.com/blog/2014/04/the-door-problem/) — verified-source-text. Live blog URL returned a stub; Wayback 2023-10-23 and the Kotaku reprint (author-permitted) were used. Essay is an analogy, not a complete spec standard. Large-studio role map does not transfer 1:1 to solo.
- [The Level Design Book - How to make a level](https://book.leveldesignbook.com/process/overview) — verified-source-text. Process page read. CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 living document, self-labelled under construction and shooter-weighted. Not authored by Steve Lee. Structure may change.
- [A Game Design Vocabulary](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/a-game-design/9780133155181) — source-claim. Publisher listings, sample pages and a 2015 practitioner review only; book not read. Less operational for large live economies.
- [Animation in Lyra Sample Game in Unreal Engine](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/animation-in-lyra-sample-game-in-unreal-engine?lang=en-US) — verified-source-text. Official docs extract. Unreal AnimBP commands wrap portable ideas; does not define contact-state art.
- [Sigma Protocol game-development adaptation (architecture decision and integration contract)](../SIGMA-GAME-ADAPTATION.md) — proposed-adaptation. Our proposal, not an external practitioner method. Nothing here proves a game is good. Every phase's evidence must come from an authorized external experiment and a human review, never from the existence of a document.
