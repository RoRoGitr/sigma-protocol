# Discovery

## When to Use

- Running a discovery / consult call and you need the question set + sequence.
- A lead asked "how much is it?" before you've established value — the qualify-first gate fires here.
- You're prepping a sales meeting (company snapshot, attendee roles, talking points, landmines).
- You need to surface and quantify the prospect's real pain before pitching anything.
- You want to map a prospect to where they actually are (supply- vs demand-constrained; $100M-roadmap stage).

Discovery is upstream of the close. The work here is **diagnosis** — expose the pain, confirm the budget, and let the prospect talk themselves into the problem. "When you ask the right questions, clients sell themselves."

## Frameworks

### The Qualify-First Gate (run BEFORE any custom proposal)

NEVER build a custom proposal, mood board, or scope-of-work before budget is confirmed. The proposal is paid expertise, not a free sample.

```
Inbound asks "how much is it?" before understanding value
  → Do NOT open the laptop and craft the branded proposal.
  → Ask the four words first: "What's your budget?"
  → Establish value + confirm they're serious.
  → ONLY THEN propose.
```

If they cannot or will not state a budget, they are not yet qualified — keep diagnosing, do not produce free work.

### Pain mirroring → cumulative-pain math

1. **Mirror the pain so specifically it feels "gently exposed."** Describe their problem back in their own language before you describe your solution. This is where rapport begins — not "hey queen."
2. **Do the cumulative-pain math.** Convert duration into a vivid number: "13 years of drawing brows = 4,745 days of frustration. I solve it in two hours." The math makes the cost of the status quo concrete and makes your price look small.
3. **Diagnose root-first.** Solve the upstream cause, not the surface symptom.

### Pre-close reveal

Before ending the consult, ask: **"Is there anything preventing you from booking?"** This surfaces the unasked question — the real objection — while you can still address it live.

### SPIN questions (repo `meeting-prep.md`)

Ten discovery questions across the four SPIN stages:

**Situation**
1. "Can you walk me through how your team currently handles [PROCESS]?"
2. "What tools or systems are you using today for [FUNCTION]?"

**Problem**
3. "What's the biggest challenge you're facing with [AREA]?"
4. "How is [PAIN_POINT] impacting [SPECIFIC_METRIC — revenue, time, team morale]?"

**Implication**
5. "If this challenge isn't addressed in the next [TIMEFRAME], what happens?"
6. "How does this affect other teams or departments?"

**Need-Payoff**
7. "If you could solve [PAIN_POINT], what would that mean for [TEAM/BUSINESS]?"
8. "What does success look like for this initiative?"

**Decision Process**
9. "Who else would need to be involved in evaluating a solution like this?"
10. "What's your timeline for making a decision on this?"

### Meeting-prep brief structure (repo)

Before any discovery call, fill: company snapshot · attendee profiles (buying role: economic buyer / champion / evaluator / end-user / blocker) · business situation (current state + pain points + quantified business impact + trigger events) · competitive context · 5–7 talking points · 10 discovery questions · 5 expected objections + prepared responses · success metrics · **competitive landmines** (e.g. "previous vendor burned them → emphasize a trust-builder"; "champion may lose influence → map other allies").

### Hormozi supply-vs-demand-constrained diagnostic

Add one diagnostic question to every discovery call: **is the prospect supply-constrained or demand-constrained?** It maps them faster than generic SPIN.

| Constraint | Symptom | What they actually need (their real pain) |
|------------|---------|-------------------------------------------|
| **Supply-constrained** | Booked out, can't deliver more, packed calendar | Raise price → change delivery ratio (1:1 → 1:many) → productize → hire |
| **Demand-constrained** | Open slots, leads not converting | Fix attribution → ads → content |

A packed calendar is "the market screaming your price is too low" — a supply-constraint signal, not a flex.

### $100M Scaling Roadmap — stage mapping

Map the prospect onto the 10-stage × 8-function roadmap (product, marketing, sales, CS, IT, recruiting, HR, finance). Each stage carries a constraint symptom and the single action that graduates it. Use the stage to surface the real pain and to position the offer at the right altitude — don't pitch a $1,000 thing to a stage-0 owner with a $100 problem, or a $100 thing to a stage-5 owner with a $1,000 problem.

## Anti-Patterns

- Building a branded proposal / mood board before budget is confirmed (the proposal is paid work, not a free sample).
- Opening with "hey queen" / generic rapport instead of mirroring their specific pain.
- Pitching features before diagnosing — "Questions close, features push."
- Solving the surface symptom instead of the upstream root cause.
- Ending the consult without asking "is anything preventing you from booking?"
- Treating "how much is it?" as a buying signal instead of a qualify-first trigger.
- Running generic SPIN without the supply-vs-demand constraint diagnostic.
- Letting the prospect leave without you knowing their decision process and timeline.

## Quality Gates

- [ ] **Budget confirmed BEFORE any custom proposal** (qualify-first gate passed).
- [ ] Prospect's pain **mirrored back** in their own words before any solution talk.
- [ ] Cumulative-pain math computed (duration → vivid number) where applicable.
- [ ] Root cause identified, not just the surface symptom.
- [ ] All four SPIN stages covered (Situation / Problem / Implication / Need-Payoff).
- [ ] Decision process + timeline + economic buyer identified.
- [ ] Supply- vs demand-constrained diagnosis recorded.
- [ ] Pre-close "is anything preventing you from booking?" asked before the consult ends.


## Transcript-grounded upgrade
**NEPQ phase ladder (run in order; the sale lives below the first answer):**
connection → situation → **problem-awareness** → solution-awareness → **consequence** → commitment (src: nSeCECTxoq8, o9knCDfS3UU).

**Answer-depth scoring:** rate each key answer 1–3 (1 = surface/reflex, 3 = root-cause with emotion). A 1 means
**knock again** — ask the next layer before moving on; fast generic questions get generic answers (src: U2w7SQ7NEUQ).

**Capture every call:** current state · desired state · the blocker between them · the **trigger** (what made now
the time) · the **consequence** of inaction. This is the gap that makes price reasonable.

**Stakeholder prevention:** surface other deciders EARLY ("besides you, who weighs in?") so the partner/boss
objection never appears late — hand to `stakeholder-authority-mapping`.

**Mechanics:** the rep talks **≤30%** (verbal pacing, silence after intentional questions); tone carries intent,
so slow the key questions down (src: IrXp2hDJQVk). Map the transcript to CRM fields via `call-intelligence-writer`;
when a late objection surfaces, switch to `objection-secondary-discovery` (ask, don't rebut).
