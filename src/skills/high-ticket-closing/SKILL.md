# High-Ticket Closing

## When to Use

Pull this when running, scripting, or reviewing a high-ticket consult or sales call — anything where a service or transformation package ($1,500+) gets closed on a call or in a DM. Also use it to diagnose a low close rate: the call is a "heartbeat check," not a persuasion event, so a low close rate is usually an upstream content failure, not a call failure (see `content-to-client`).

**Core thesis:** **Questions close, features push.** When you ask the right questions, clients sell themselves. The close is leadership and service — clients want to be led.

## Core DNA (the operators behind this)

- **Sheila Bella** (*The Respect Method*) — her Socratic close, 8 Psychological Milestones, the 7-Step Respect Method call sequence, and the surgical word-swaps that turn a hoping question into a leading one. Conviction isn't practiced in a mirror — it's installed as a system.
- **Alex Hormozi** (*$100M Offers*) — the **90-day activation plan as delivery proof**: bake a concrete day-90 onboarding-win milestone into the close so the buyer sees the path, not just the price.

## Scripts & Patterns

### The 7-Step Respect Method Sequence (call mechanics, reel DY8wDr4hbIi)

The ordered backbone of every high-ticket call. "These seven steps turn price shoppers into premium clients without you apologizing, bending, justifying, shrinking, or auditioning."

1. **Reset the frame** — re-establish that this is two adults making a transaction, not an audition. "You already have this job. You don't need to try out for it."
2. **Establish authority** — show the pattern (helped many, overcame it yourself), not the resume.
3. **Present the real problem** — the upstream cause, named so specifically the client feels "gently exposed."
4. **Present the right solution** — the transformation, not the session list. "They don't want your information, they want your transformation — you are the product demo."
5. **Eliminate objections** — translate and dissolve (hand off to `objection-handling`).
6. **Confirm the decision** — the assumptive two-option close (below).
7. **Transfer ownership** — take the deposit now; the client now owns the outcome.

### The Socratic Close — 3 power questions

"Questions close, features push." The three that make the client sell themselves:

- "**What about what you have now would you never give up?**"
- "**What would need to be true for this to be an easy yes?**"
- "**What's the cost of staying where you are?**"

### The 8 Psychological Milestones (the close confirms these passed)

The buyer must pass all eight before the close lands. If the call stalls, find the missing milestone:

1. She knows my problem better than I do.
2. She's overcome it herself.
3. She's helped thousands (1 = luck, 10 = pattern, 100 = competence).
4. I know / like / trust her.
5. I want to be like her.
6. She explains it logically.
7. Even if I don't like her, she can help me.
8. I can't get this anywhere else.

### Word-swaps (verbatim, reel DZJbfeJO8Bz) — one hopes, the other leads

| Begging phrase | Leading replacement |
|---|---|
| "So what do you think?" | "**What questions do you have before we get started?**" |
| "I can send you more info" | "**I'd rather walk you through it right now so you don't figure this out alone**" |
| "Let me know" | "**Here's your next step**" |
| "Price" | "**Investment**" |

Kill **"let me know," "take your time,"** and any **unprompted discounting** — "I can probably do a little discount" tells them your price was never real.

### Cost-of-inaction

> "The price is what you pay now; the **cost** is what you pay later. When they do the math, your price stops looking expensive." — Sheila Bella

Cumulative-pain math makes it concrete: "You've drawn your brows for 13 years — that's 4,745 days of frustration. I solve it in two hours."

### Two-option assumptive close + take the deposit now

- Not "take your time" → "**I have Thursday at 7 or Friday at 10 — which works better?**"
- **Take the deposit on the phone, now.** "The minute you hang up, kids need things, notifications go off — she won't book later." Sell packages, prepaid; money before the appointment kills no-shows.
- Pre-close reveal before ending the consult: "**Is there anything preventing you from booking?**"

### SPIN discovery on the call (lifted framework)

Sequence the questions so the client builds the case themselves:

- **Situation** — "Walk me through how you currently handle [process]."
- **Problem** — "What's the biggest challenge you're facing with [area]?"
- **Implication** — "If this isn't addressed in the next [timeframe], what happens?"
- **Need-payoff** — "If you could solve [pain], what would that mean for you?"

Decision-process questions before you close: "Who else needs to be involved?" and "What's your timeline?"

### Hormozi 90-day activation plan as delivery proof

In step 4 (present the solution) and again in the close, include a **90-day activation plan**: name the concrete onboarding win the client will hit by day 90. This shows the path to the result, de-risks the spend, and pre-loads retention (the day-90 cohort milestone where churn drops). The plan is the bridge from "your price" to "my result."

## Anti-Patterns (what to refuse)

- Pitching features instead of asking questions. Features push; questions close.
- "Let me know," "take your time," "what do you think?" — hoping language that hands the lead the exit.
- Unprompted discounting — it announces your price was never real.
- Ending the call without taking a deposit or booking a specific time.
- Treating the consult as a persuasion event — it's a heartbeat check on a buyer your content already pre-sold.
- Selling sessions/time instead of the transformation. You get paid for the value you bring to time.

## Quality Gates

- [ ] Ran the 7-step sequence in order; identified which step the call stalled at if it did.
- [ ] Close was built from questions (Socratic), not a feature dump.
- [ ] Verified all 8 Psychological Milestones passed before confirming the decision.
- [ ] Every begging phrase swapped for its leading replacement; "price" → "investment."
- [ ] Cost-of-inaction quantified so the price stopped looking expensive.
- [ ] Closed with the two-option assumptive close AND took the deposit / booked a specific time.
- [ ] A 90-day activation milestone named as part of the offer (delivery proof + retention).

### The Seven Buying Beliefs tracker (gordon's spine — `src: c-0ys26n-1E, fsascX_GkwE`)

Before a buyer pays, seven beliefs must be intact. Run them as a checklist on the live call; a broken belief surfaces later as a "price/think/partner" objection, so find it now and repair it before the close. This is the belief layer beneath the v1.0 8 Psychological Milestones — milestones build the *person's* trust in you; beliefs build the *decision's* logic.

| # | belief the buyer must hold | broken-belief tell (what it looks like) | repair move | persona |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **I have a real problem** worth solving now | minimizes pain, "it's not that bad", flat energy | deepen with "How long has that been going on for?" → expand duration/cost | miner |
| 2 | **The cost of staying the same is high** | likes the idea, no urgency | "What happens if nothing changes?" — make inaction the competing cost (`src: mDWUpuumAuo, arCrKEkyXgA`) | miner |
| 3 | **This mechanism is the right vehicle** | compares to other methods, "couldn't I just…" | Pitch As Failure Explanation: why prior attempts failed, why this mechanism is different (`src: f8mzLaf3IPg`) | gordon |
| 4 | **This company/operator can deliver it** | proof-shopping, "does this actually work?" | matched case study to the exact scar, not a generic folder (`src: KUVVShFEjdQ`) | harvey |
| 5 | **I can personally do it / get the result** | "this works but maybe not for me", self-doubt | Fear Separation — "Who's speaking, you or fear?" route to future self (`src: nSeCECTxoq8, Sximy8LRT5s`) | gordon |
| 6 | **Now is the right time** | "next quarter", "after I finish X" | capture the exact trigger that changes priority; follow the event, not a date | ross |
| 7 | **It's worth the money / I can resource it** | price/affordability wrapper | Money-Aside Diagnostic (below) — separate desire from access | gordon |

Agent rule: **diagnose the broken belief, do not answer the surface objection.** A price objection on a buyer who never believed belief #3 is a mechanism problem wearing a price mask (Objection Hierarchy: money-first only *after* belief is intact — `src: apm4-akWx0M, IJ2g-HvuEms`).

### Current-state → desired-state bridge (gordon — `src: 9N5pTrOQdCs, KPM78PmkobM, ROEqCXj_Vv4`)

Capture four things in discovery; the offer is then positioned as the **bridge across the gap**, not a product tour. Run before any pitch.

- **Where are you now?** — the current state, in the buyer's own words.
- **Where do you want to be?** — the desired state. "What were you hoping could possibly happen by working with us?" (`src: W-WHmvBtjlc`)
- **What's stopping you from getting there?** — the block (this is what the offer removes).
- **What happens if nothing changes?** — the cost of inaction (feeds belief #2 and the v1.0 cost-of-inaction math).

Verbatim anchor: **"Where are you at? Where do you want to be? What's stopping you from getting there?"** (`src: 9N5pTrOQdCs, KPM78PmkobM`). The captured gap becomes the pitch ("Made-For-Me Pitch" mirrors these exact words back — `src: nSeCECTxoq8`) and the close ("Do you see a clear vision?" tests the desired-state belief — `src: KPM78PmkobM, 7PLGJujZ3go`).

### Certainty gate before payment (gordon — `src: 7PLGJujZ3go, jM9l5DFrpQE, KUVVShFEjdQ, nSeCECTxoq8`)

**Do not send a payment link or ask for money until certainty is built.** Payment failure usually started as belief failure. Gate it:

1. **Belief check:** "Do you feel like our process works?" (`src: KUVVShFEjdQ, nSeCECTxoq8`). A weak "yeah, I think so" is a fail — do not proceed.
2. **Vision check:** "Do you see a clear vision?" (desired-state belief intact — `src: KPM78PmkobM, 7PLGJujZ3go`).
3. **Certainty isolation:** when below a clean 10/10 → **"What do you think is keeping you from being less than 100% certain?"** (`src: IJ2g-HvuEms, Sximy8LRT5s`). Isolate the one blocker, set it aside, double tie-down: "if that's handled, are you in?"
4. **No-risk bridge:** "if it's a no-risk decision and you're 10 out of 10, I'm confused on what would hold you back from just moving forward" (`src: IJ2g-HvuEms, vgZrDvLcPIQ`).

Only when certainty is real do you reveal price. Selling against a weak yes manufactures refunds and chargebacks (see refund-risk warning below).

### Statement close + silence (belfort — `src: U2w7SQ7NEUQ, wOIZv2Eef3Q`)

At price reveal: **state the investment tied to the outcome/KPI, say it calmly, then stop talking.**

- **"The first person who speaks after you reveal price loses."** (`src: U2w7SQ7NEUQ, wOIZv2Eef3Q`) — the silence demonstrates certainty; filling it signals doubt and invites discounting.
- Deliver flat and concise (belfort tonality: confident, controlled). No nervous justification, no "but we can be flexible," no feature re-list.
- Then move into the close paths: **"Which one lights you up the most?"** (option close — `src: U2w7SQ7NEUQ`) or the assumptive payment transition **"What's your billing address?"** (`src: KPM78PmkobM, wOIZv2Eef3Q`).

Anti-pattern: the seller who keeps talking after price. If you over-explain post-reveal, route to belfort's discipline — the deal is stuck because *you* won't stop, not because the buyer won't start.

### Payment-plan-after-commitment rule (harvey — `src: apm4-akWx0M, IJ2g-HvuEms`)

**Pitch full price first. Financing is a tool used only *after* commitment, never the lead.**

- **"When you pitch, you pitch like your full price."** (`src: apm4-akWx0M`) — leading with the monthly number anchors low and tells the buyer the real price was never real.
- Sequence: build value → reveal full investment → statement-close silence → handle the one blocker → *only then*, if cash timing is the live constraint, offer structure.
- Paid-in-full incentive comes **after** the yes, as a reward, not a rescue: **"Did I mention you actually get a bit of a discount for paying in full?"** (`src: apm4-akWx0M`).
- Cash inventory is done **after buy-in**, ethically: "I don't want you not to be able to eat" — surface real cash/credit/incoming funds to build a safe plan, never to pressure (`src: apm4-akWx0M, IJ2g-HvuEms`). This ties to the v1.0 take-the-deposit-now move: deposit captures commitment; the plan structures the balance.

### Refund-risk warning — when reps push past consent

A close built by overriding the buyer's "no" produces a sale that **refunds, charges back, or churns**. Stop and flag when any of these fire:

- **Weak yes / flat energy at payment.** "I sense a bit of hesitation there — where's that coming from?" (`src: nSeCECTxoq8, q8CHXefn7B4`). Do not proceed to the payment link on a weak yes — repair belief first (certainty gate above).
- **Fear, not desire, is driving the yes.** Separate them ("Who's speaking — you or fear?"). A fear-driven buyer buys to end the call, then refunds when the fear lifts (`src: nSeCECTxoq8, Sximy8LRT5s`).
- **Buyer can't actually afford it.** Pushing past a real cash constraint = "I don't want you not to be able to eat" exists *because* selling someone into money they don't have is the refund factory (`src: apm4-akWx0M`).
- **Partner objection masking unsafe pressure.** If "I need to talk to my partner" is real co-decision, get the partner on the phone — do not pressure a unilateral yes. Forcing it past genuine partner authority breaks trust and reverses (`src: apm4-akWx0M, NWfPXWXg-5A, Oy2UM-9XTeg`).

Doctrine (agent-run org): a refunding/charging-back close is a **net-negative** outcome — it costs processing, dispute fees, brand trust, and a poisoned referral. The certainty gate exists to keep the firm out of this. An agent that cannot build certainty **escalates or disqualifies**; it does not push. Money moves and outbound brand-facing sends stay human-gated through the approval queue.
