# Hormozi Frameworks Skill

This skill provides the Hormozi frameworks that still matter inside Sigma Protocol, especially for Step 1 and Step 1.5.

Use this skill to sharpen:
- the value equation
- the offer stack
- pricing pressure tests
- risk reversal
- the relationship between price and perceived value

Do **not** use this skill as a substitute for positioning, JTBD, or architecture. In Step 1.5 it is one framework layer, not the whole step.

## Framework 1: The Value Equation

Every feature, offer, and pricing decision must maximize perceived value:

```
Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood) / (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice)
```

### Application Checklist

- [ ] **Dream Outcome**: What is the ideal end-state the customer desires?
- [ ] **Perceived Likelihood**: How confident are they that your solution will work?
- [ ] **Time Delay**: How quickly will they see results?
- [ ] **Effort & Sacrifice**: How much work or pain is required from them?

### Scoring Guide

| Factor | Poor (1) | Average (5) | Excellent (10) |
|--------|----------|-------------|----------------|
| Dream Outcome | Generic benefit | Specific improvement | Transformational result |
| Perceived Likelihood | No proof | Some proof | Strong proof or guarantee |
| Time Delay | Months or years | Weeks | Days or immediate |
| Effort & Sacrifice | Significant work | Moderate effort | Low effort |

### Decision this should drive
- what the customer is actually buying
- which parts of the offer increase perceived value enough to justify price

## Framework 2: Grand Slam Offer Construction

A Grand Slam Offer is so good that the right buyer feels irrational saying no.

### Components

1. **Dream Outcome**
2. **Perceived Likelihood of Achievement**
3. **Time to Achievement**
4. **Effort & Sacrifice**

### The Offer Stack

Build value by stacking bonuses that solve real objections:

```markdown
## Core Offer
- [Main Product/Service] - Value: $X

## Bonus 1: Speed Bonus
- [Helps them get results faster] - Value: $X

## Bonus 2: Effort Reducer
- [Makes it easier for them] - Value: $X

## Bonus 3: Risk Reversal
- [Guarantee that removes their fear] - Value: $X

## Total Value: $X,XXX
## Your Price: $XXX
```

### Decision this should drive
- what belongs in the package
- what should be a bonus versus a core entitlement
- which objections need value-stack support

## Framework 3: The 3 Core Value Drivers

Every offer increases value by enhancing one of these:

### 1. Time
- Save time
- Buy back time
- Compress results timeline

### 2. Money
- Make more money
- Save money
- Increase ROI

### 3. Status
- Improve standing
- Recognition or achievement
- Belonging to a valued group

### Decision this should drive
- which value theme the offer leans on most heavily
- what pricing and messaging should emphasize first

## Framework 4: Risk Reversal Strategies

Remove enough risk that the buyer can say yes with less fear.

### Types of Guarantees

| Type | Example | Risk Level |
|------|---------|------------|
| **Unconditional** | "30-day money back, no questions" | Highest (you) |
| **Conditional** | "If you do X and don't get Y, refund" | Medium |
| **Anti-Guarantee** | "All sales final - this isn't for everyone" | Lowest (them) |
| **Outcome-Based** | "We'll work for free until you hit $X" | Performance |

### Decision this should drive
- which risk needs to be removed for the buyer to move now
- whether the guarantee is credible and operationally supportable

## Framework 5: Pricing Psychology

### The Pricing Ladder

1. **Free** - Lead magnet or trial
2. **Low-ticket** - Self-serve or low-touch
3. **Mid-ticket** - Guided or supported
4. **High-ticket** - Done-with-you
5. **Premium** - Done-for-you or enterprise-style support

### The Value Staircase

Price is justified by the transformation gap:

```
Current State ──────────────────────► Desired State
              [Your Solution]

Price = (Value of Desired State) - (Value of Current State)
```

### Decision this should drive
- whether the planned price feels proportionate to the perceived transformation
- whether the packaging is underpriced, overpriced, or mismatched to value delivered

## Framework 7: The 6 Levels of Getting Paid (Risk Ladder)

**Mechanic:** You're compensated in proportion to the **perceived** risk you take. Six structures, worst→best: (1) I work, you pay (employee) → (2) pay-as-we-go (contractor) → (3) pay-then-I-work (retainer/surgeon) → (4) when-X-happens-you-pay (rev-share/outcome) → (5) pay-me-to-hold-risk (guarantees/warranties) → (6) you-always-pay (toll-road/processor). The edge: make it feel risky for them, low-risk for you — you privately know the odds. Frame every proposal by its risk-transfer tier; a guarantee is itself a risk product.

> "You will be compensated in proportion to the risk you're willing to take… the people who do this well have it be very risky for other people and not risky at all for them." — `rMJIOK_FgJk` 08:15

## Framework 8: Pareto Pricing Stack (testable tier design)

**Mechanic:** Use price and packaging tiers as hypotheses, not a 5–10x rule. Define the buyer, job, delivery cost, willingness-to-pay evidence, margin, and capacity for each tier. Then test tier uptake, gross margin, conversion, refund rate, support burden, and cannibalization by cohort. Build top-down only when the higher-value tier has credible buyer and delivery evidence.

> "The only thing worse than offering a $1,000 thing to somebody with a $100 budget is offering a $100 thing to somebody with a $1,000 budget. First you lose 100 bucks; second you lose 900." — `ZuJryiwxjDw` 06:29

**Triggers:** 'pareto pricing', 'pricing stack', '5x tier', '10x tier', 'price tiers', 'sell to the rich'.

## Framework 9: Retention Cohort Framework

**Mechanic:** Churn is cohort-dependent, but no universal day-90 or month-6 curve applies. Segment retention by acquisition source, plan, persona, tenure, product behavior, and support exposure. Identify the earliest measurable activation event associated with retained customers; test onboarding and community interventions against that baseline. Use renewal timing based on actual contract and cohort behavior.

> "Don't ask 'how do I make churn go down' but 'how do I get someone to stay past day 90?'" — `-j8_YCWZ05Q` 04:29

**Triggers:** 'retention cohort', 'churn', 'day 90', 'month 6', 'keep customers', '10 true regulars', 'renewal trigger'. (Full marketing build: `retention-engine` skill.)

## Framework 10: Value Per Second (Subtraction > Addition for retention)

**Mechanic:** Extra features can increase cognitive load or dilute a clear outcome. Treat that as a product hypothesis: use interviews, feature adoption, time-to-value, support contacts, and retention cohorts before removing or adding delivery elements. Diagnostics: "If I deleted everything, what one thing would you fight for?" / "Which feature would you not miss?" Optimize for **value per second, not seconds of value.**

> "Value per second, not seconds of value. Someone is more likely to churn because you added more stuff — now they feel they're using not enough of it." — `-j8_YCWZ05Q` 12:02

**Triggers:** 'value per second', 'subtract features', 'planet fitness', 'feature bloat', 'simplify offer for retention'.

## Framework 11: The Trim and Stack Method

**Mechanic:** Offer surgery, not new-build. Inventory every deliverable, then cut anything that wouldn't justify the full price **on its own**. Live demo: a $25K coaching offer → cut Slack (40% of time, lowest-rated) + weekly calls (lowest-rated) → kept deep-dive onboarding + quarterly 1:1s + 2 annual getaways → same $25K, 5x capacity. Use it against "too much / too complicated" objections — **subtract** low-value elements instead of discounting; it preserves price.

> "Each thing we add on its own should be worth the price of the whole thing. Simplicity is the ultimate in business." — `N5MExtki_VI` 13:26

**Triggers:** 'trim and stack', 'cut the offer', 'too complicated', 'offer surgery', 'simplify the package'.

## Framework 12: The Fusion Reactor (customers build your ad machine)

**Mechanic:** Engineer the core experience to auto-produce ad creative — e.g. a $2,500 **rebate** for clients who record a testimonial video + grant usage rights. Loop: experience → documentation → ads → next customer. Only one marketing push is needed to start the reactor. Copy rule: once copy converts, **freeze it** — only rotate creative. Bake a testimonial-rebate trigger into onboarding to close the acquisition loop.

> "I want the normal function of the business to create the marketing for the business. I want a fusion reactor." — `N5MExtki_VI` 27:52

**Triggers:** 'fusion reactor', 'testimonial rebate', 'customers create marketing', 'social proof flywheel', 'freeze winning copy'.

## Framework 13: The 1-1-1 Rule

**Mechanic:** One product, one avatar, one channel until $1M. Every pre-$1M diversification is a tax on speed. A prospect (or you) selling to everyone = misaligned avatar = low-close signal. Kill early multi-channel scatter; concentrate until the funnel converts.

> "Sell one product or service to one avatar on one channel until you make $1 million. That's it." — `jfW6gL6hKhk` 08:36

**Triggers:** '1-1-1 rule', 'one product one avatar one channel', 'first $1M', 'stop diversifying', 'too many channels'.

## Framework 14: CAC-to-LTV Stall Predictor (cohort-economics test)

**Mechanic:** Do not treat a price band as a universal SMB dead zone or use it to disqualify buyers. Model each segment’s contribution margin, payback period, retention curve, delivery capacity, and cash requirement. Compare higher-ticket, lower-touch, annual-prepay, and packaging alternatives as testable hypotheses. Change price, delivery, or qualification only when cohort evidence improves the model.

> "Drop low, go high. The middle's just a dead zone where everyone dies." — `jqo0lVveh98` 12:38

> Triage companion (supply- vs demand-constrained): "Every business needs to know the core actions that increase how much money it makes. If you can't define that, your employees sure can't." — `jqo0lVveh98` 03:00

## When to Apply

1. **Step 1**: Apply the Value Equation to pressure-test the product concept.
2. **Step 1.5**: Use Value Equation, Grand Slam Offer, Value Drivers, Risk Reversal, and Pricing Psychology as the core Hormozi layer.
3. **Step 9**: Use CLOSER more directly in landing-page and conversion work.
4. **Pricing Decisions**: Use these frameworks to pressure-test value, not to bypass positioning or JTBD analysis.

## MCP Integration

When researching competitors or pricing context:
- use EXA or Ref to find competitor pricing and packaging
- compare the value stack and guarantee strategy, not just the headline price
- record where you are inferring from market patterns instead of direct evidence

## When NOT to Use

- Not for technical implementation — business strategy and offer design only
- Not for B2B enterprise sales cycles — optimized for B2C and SMB offers
- Not for non-profit or community projects — commercial value equation focus
- Not for product design — business model and pricing framework
