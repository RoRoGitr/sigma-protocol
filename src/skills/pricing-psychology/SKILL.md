# Pricing Psychology

## When to Use

Pull this when setting or raising a price, sizing offer tiers, or fixing a number you've quietly negotiated down on yourself. This is the **sales-side psychology of pricing** — posture, buyer-sorting, tier architecture, risk-transfer. For COGS / margin / unit-economics math, use the finance skills; this skill governs the *number* and *who it filters for*, not the spreadsheet.

**Core thesis:** **Your price is your posture.** One number repels money, the other attracts it — and the only difference is the number you have the audacity to put on yourself. Raising prices is a marketing strategy: it filters for the clients you want.

## Evidence boundary

Treat these practitioner scripts as optional lenses. Emotional discomfort, wealth labels and creator anecdotes are not proof of willingness to pay. Use buyer research, costs, margin, alternatives and measured demand before proposing a price change. Quotes and earnings claims require source verification before external use.

## Scripts & Patterns

### Stop rounding down (verbatim, reel DZGrzn1MvCG)

> "You were about to charge 500 but typed 450 because it felt like a lot — **to you, not them. You negotiated with yourself before they even opened the message.** You're not giving a discount, you're giving away confidence… The grocery store doesn't round down for your feelings. That's $6,000 a year because of a feeling. The real number — the one before the fear kicked in — **send it.**"

Rule: charge the real number — the one before the fear kicked in. Rounding down is a self-inflicted pay cut.

### The 6-Question Price-Raise Diagnostic (verbatim, reel DZDuMZdSERV)

Signals to investigate — none alone justifies raising a price. "Under-pricing isn't humble. It's helpless."

1. **Booked out two weeks or more?** "A packed calendar is the market screaming that your price is too low."
2. **Haven't raised in 12 months?** "Standing still on price isn't loyalty — it's a pay cut you handed yourself."
3. **Saying your price out loud makes you nauseous?** "That's not fear, it's your number finally being correct." (If it slides out easy with no flinch, it's also too low — raise it.)
4. **Client says "that's so affordable"?** "She just told you she would have paid more."
5. **Waiting until you feel more confident?** "Confidence doesn't show up first and then you raise — it comes sprinting after you raise."
6. **Secretly resent your favorite client a little when she pays?** "That resentment is your worth leaking out the sides."

### Poor-vs-Wealthy Buyer Contrast (verbatim, reels DY3Y25wJQsT / DZEiLiksZPR)

ICP-sorting by how you price and who it attracts:

| Selling to the poor | Selling to the wealthy |
|---|---|
| Price on what you think they can afford | Price on transformation — "what is the result worth?" |
| Go $10 under the competitor → race to the bottom | Don't even know the competitor's price; set your own standard |
| Charge by the hour (faster = less money) | Charge for the outcome (45 min or 2 hrs, the result is the result) |
| Discount before anyone asks (pre-negotiating) | No sales, no deals — "there's just a price" |
| "Book me please, I'm broke, I need this" | "Are you sure you're ready for this?" |

"You price based on transformation… not your insecurity, not what the girl down the street charges."

### Whales vs Goldfish math

Two client types only. $20k/mo = **1,000 goldfish @ $20** *or* **10 whales @ $2,000.** "The clients you lose by raising prices are the ones you want to lose." Build the offer pyramid: bottom (single sessions, hustle) → middle (bundles) → top (transformation packages $1,500+). "$1,500 brow packages, 34 clients/mo = $50k/mo, 3 days a week."

### Veblen / Brand Premium

A luxury price signals higher perceived demand. "Clients pay for the brand, not the service. You can be right or you can be rich." The premium price itself is the marketing.

### Hormozi — 6 Levels of Getting Paid (Risk Ladder)

You're paid in proportion to the **perceived** risk you take. Six structures, worst → best:

1. I work, you pay (employee)
2. Pay-as-we-go (contractor)
3. Pay-then-I-work (retainer / surgeon)
4. When-X-happens-you-pay (rev-share / outcome)
5. Pay-me-to-hold-risk (guarantees / warranties)
6. You-always-pay (toll-road / processor)

> "You will be compensated in proportion to the risk you're willing to take… the people who do this well have it be very risky for other people and not risky at all for them." — Alex Hormozi

Edge: make it feel risky for them, low-risk for you (you privately know the odds). Frame every proposal by its risk-transfer tier; a guarantee is a *risk product* you sell.

### Hormozi — Pareto Pricing Stack (5–10x per tier)

Treat tier spacing and uptake as hypotheses. Design each tier around a buyer need, delivery cost and willingness-to-pay evidence; measure conversion, margin and cannibalization. There is no universal multiplier or revenue-doubling rule.

> "The only thing worse than offering a $1,000 thing to somebody with a $100 budget is offering a $100 thing to somebody with a $1,000 budget. First you lose 100 bucks; second you lose 900." — Alex Hormozi

Self-check before every quote: **"Are we pitching a $100 thing to a $1,000 buyer?"** Wealth math: the top 1% holds >32% of US household wealth — "selling to the rich" is arithmetic, not greed.

## Anti-Patterns (what to refuse)

- Rounding down — negotiating with yourself before the client opens the message.
- Pricing on what you guess the buyer can afford instead of on the transformation.
- Going $10 under the competitor — the race to the bottom only the client wins.
- Charging by the hour, so getting faster makes you poorer.
- Running flash sales / pre-emptive discounts nobody asked for.
- Treating a preset tier multiplier as buyer evidence.
- Pitching a low-ticket thing to a high-budget buyer (you lose the bigger margin).

## Quality Gates

- [ ] Price is the real number (pre-fear) — no self-rounding-down.
- [ ] Diagnostic signals were checked against buyer and business evidence before proposing a price change.
- [ ] Price set on transformation/outcome, not hourly and not on assumed buyer wallet.
- [ ] Offer architected as whales-not-goldfish (top tier $1,500+ transformation package).
- [ ] Each tier has a defined buyer, cost/margin model and testable price hypothesis.
- [ ] Proposal framed by its risk-transfer tier; guarantee treated as a sold risk product.
- [ ] Checked we're not pitching a low-ticket thing to a high-budget buyer.


## Transcript-grounded upgrade
**Price is solved by cost-of-inaction + payment architecture, not a discount reflex.** Runtime moves:

- **Price-vs-cost distinction:** "Are you referring to the price, or the cost?" — separate the sticker from the
  cost of staying the same (src: U2w7SQ7NEUQ).
- **Money-aside diagnostic:** "Money aside, would you do it?" — splits a desire problem from a funds problem.
  Real desire → solve structure; weak desire → back to discovery (src: apm4-akWx0M).
- **Cost-of-inaction stack:** quantify what the unsolved problem costs per week/month before any number lands.
- **Full-price-before-payment-plan rule:** pitch the full price like you mean it; financing is a *post-commitment*
  tool, never the opener (src: apm4-akWx0M, IJ2g-HvuEms via `payment-commitment-resolver`).
- **Same-price competitor comparison:** when "X is cheaper," don't drop price — re-anchor on outcome + risk
  removed so the offers stop sounding interchangeable (src: WZx-hsb-H9A).
- **Budget-timing split:** "budget right now" is one of three things — hard budget absence, a timing constraint,
  or a value gap. Diagnose which before responding.
- **No fake revenue/guarantee rule:** never promise income or a specific return to make the price feel safe —
  it creates refund/dispute risk and breaks governance (src: vgZrDvLcPIQ, c-0ys26n-1E).
