# Game adaptation — Step 3: UX Design

Generated from catalog 1.0.0. Canonical source: `steps/step-3-ux-design`.

Treatment: **adapt** — Experience goals, journeys, information architecture and Design DNA stay. For a game the journey must include the first session, the fail state and its recovery, optional content and the ending. Readability is treated as a perception problem (contrast, silhouette, focus, consistent mapping, never colour alone) before it is a visual-identity problem. Direction studies are allowed as constraints; final asset production waits for proofs.

## Creator questions

- **first-session**: Walk the first ten minutes: what does the player learn, by doing what, without a text dump?
- **fail-recovery**: How does the player fail, know they failed, and recover? Where is 'stuck' and 'lost' handled?
- **readability**: At the real play camera or view, how does the player tell interactable from decorative, danger from safe, path from wall, and focus from background?
- **hud-philosophy**: Diegetic, minimal, or full HUD? What must never be hidden behind an option?
- **feedback-language**: What are the visual, audio and haptic acknowledgements for the core verb and for every decision?

## activities

- Run the standard UX design with game journeys added (first session, fail, recovery, optional, ending)
- Produce direction studies (palette, silhouette language, reference board) as constraints, not final art
- Define the readability test at play scale
- Scope accessibility questions alongside experience goals (input, timing, reading load)

## reviewCriteria

- Journeys cover first session, fail, recovery, optional content and ending
- Readability rules are testable at the play camera or view, not only in a style frame
- No final-quality asset is commissioned by this step

## revisionRules

- Changing an experience goal re-opens the journeys that served it
- Changing the art mode re-opens direction studies
- Accessibility scoping answers that reveal timers, quick-time events or colour-only signals are design changes, not polish tickets

## Sources and limits

- [The Gamer's Brain - GDC talks and book record](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XIpDLa585ao) — source-claim. Talk metadata and transcript snippets; not frame-reviewed. Books not read. Neuroscience framing can be over-claimed if used as authority rather than as testable guidelines. Fortnite-scale UX org does not transfer to solo.
- [Xbox Accessibility Guidelines V3.2](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/xbox/accessibility/guidelines) — verified-source-text. Official Learn page read (v3.2 published 2023-06-08; page updated 2026-08). Xbox-example-heavy. Explicitly not a legal compliance checklist. Not every guideline body was fully read.
- [The Level Design Book - How to make a level](https://book.leveldesignbook.com/process/overview) — verified-source-text. Process page read. CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 living document, self-labelled under construction and shooter-weighted. Not authored by Steve Lee. Structure may change.
- [Game Feel: A Game Designer's Guide to Virtual Sensation](https://www.taylorfrancis.com/books/mono/10.1201/9781482267334/game-feel-steve-swink) — source-claim. Publisher pages and Library of Congress table of contents only; book body not read. Does not apply cleanly to turn-based, menu-driven or non-spatial games. 2008 input-hardware examples.
- [Sigma Protocol game-development adaptation (architecture decision and integration contract)](../SIGMA-GAME-ADAPTATION.md) — proposed-adaptation. Our proposal, not an external practitioner method. Nothing here proves a game is good. Every phase's evidence must come from an authorized external experiment and a human review, never from the existence of a document.
